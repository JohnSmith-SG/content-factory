# AI Tells Checklist

A living list of patterns that make writing sound AI-generated. Add to it whenever you catch a new pattern in your own drafts. Referenced by `governance-coach`.

Inspired by Every's editorial team (Kate Lee's top-edit skill).

> **Область действия: английский текст (Facebook).** Этот чек-лист написан
> для английского. Пункт 12 («Em Dash Overuse») **не применяй к русскому
> тексту (Telegram)** — в русском языке тире естественно и не является
> ИИ-маркером, см. `context/writing-principles.md`, правило 15. Для
> русской версии используй отдельный блок ниже.

---

## 1. Vague Pronoun Openers

**Pattern:** Sentences starting with "This" or "That" without a following noun.

**Bad:** "This is important because..."
**Good:** "This shift is important because..."

---

## 2. Correlative Constructions

**Pattern:** Overuse of balanced pairs: "not only...but also", "both...and", "whether...or".

**Bad:** "AI is not only transforming how we work, but also reshaping how we think."
**Good:** "AI is transforming work. It's also starting to reshape how we think."

These structures sound formal and written-by-committee. Break them up.

---

## 3. Formulaic Transitions

**Pattern:** Generic transition words that add no meaning.

**Kill list:** Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, In addition, It's worth noting, Notably, Importantly, Indeed, In fact, That being said, To be sure, Needless to say.

**What to do instead:** Cut the transition entirely. If the paragraph flows logically, you don't need a transition word. If it doesn't flow, the problem is structure, not vocabulary.

---

## 4. Hedging Phrases

**Pattern:** Weakening claims to avoid commitment.

**Kill list:** It could be argued that, It seems like, It appears that, One might say, Perhaps, It's possible that, To some extent, In many ways, It's not unreasonable to suggest.

**What to do instead:** Take a position. If you're not confident enough to state it directly, either find evidence or cut the claim.

---

## 5. Marketing Speak

**Pattern:** Hype words that add no information.

**Kill list:** Game-changing, Cutting-edge, Revolutionary, Groundbreaking, Innovative, Transformative, Unprecedented, Paradigm shift (unless specifically about Kuhn), Synergy, Leverage (as a verb meaning "use"), Unlock, Empower.

**What to do instead:** Be specific. "This reduced processing time by 40%" beats "This was a game-changing innovation."

---

## 6. Signpost Phrases

**Pattern:** Announcing what you're about to do instead of doing it.

**Kill list:** In this article we will explore..., Let's dive in, Let's unpack this, Without further ado, As we'll see below, As mentioned earlier, It goes without saying, Let me explain, Here's the thing.

**What to do instead:** Just make the point. The reader doesn't need a tour guide.

---

## 7. False Drama Fragments

**Pattern:** Short sentence fragments used for artificial emphasis.

**Bad:** "The implications? Massive."
**Bad:** "The result? A complete transformation."
**Bad:** "And that changes everything."

**When fragments work:** When they're rare and earned. One per essay, max. And only when the content truly warrants the emphasis.

---

## 8. Artificial Triads

**Pattern:** Lists of exactly three items when two or four would be more natural. AI loves threes.

**Bad:** "This requires patience, persistence, and perspective."
**Good:** "This requires patience and persistence." (if that's all you mean)

**Test:** Does the third item add a genuinely distinct idea, or is it padding?

---

## 9. Balanced "On the Other Hand" Structures

**Pattern:** Perfectly balanced pros-and-cons that refuse to take a position.

**Bad:** "On one hand, AI offers tremendous potential. On the other hand, it poses significant risks."
**Good:** "AI's potential is real. The risks are being systematically underestimated."

**What to do instead:** Take a side. Balanced doesn't mean intelligent.

---

## 10. Consultant-Speak

**Pattern:** Business jargon that signals nothing.

**Kill list:** At the end of the day, Moving the needle, Value proposition, Low-hanging fruit, Circle back, Deep dive, Stakeholders (outside of actual corporate contexts), Best practices, Going forward, Key takeaway, Actionable insights.

**What to do instead:** Use plain language. Say what you mean.

---

## 11. Banned AI Words

**Pattern:** Words that immediately signal AI-generated text. They sound polished but say nothing.

**Kill list:** Leverage (as a verb), Comprehensive, Pivotal, Delve, Showcase, Intricate, Landscape, Tapestry, Transformative, Paradigm, Robust, Seamless, Unlock, Dynamic.

**What to do instead:** Use the plain word. "Use" not "leverage." "Thorough" not "comprehensive." "Important" not "pivotal." If the plain word sounds boring, the sentence probably doesn't need the adjective at all.

---

## 12. Em Dash Overuse

**Pattern:** Using em dashes as a crutch for connecting thoughts. AI text relies on em dashes to stitch clauses together instead of committing to sentence structure.

**Bad:** "The tools were getting better — month over month — and that scared me."
**Good:** "The tools were getting better, month over month. That scared me."

**Rule:** Avoid em dashes. Use periods, commas, parentheses, or start a new sentence.

---

## 13. Formula Phrases

**Pattern:** Structural phrases that AI uses to create artificial balance or emphasis.

**Kill list:** "Not just X, but also Y" / "In today's world" / "It's important to note" / "A key takeaway is" / "Let's explore."

**What to do instead:** Cut the formula and say the thing directly.

---

## 14. Forced Symmetry

**Pattern:** Paragraphs of identical length, mirrored sentence structures, sections that feel templated.

**Bad:** "Less building the model, more checking the output. Less time as the engine, more time steering. Less production, more judgment."

**Test:** Read three consecutive paragraphs. Do they have the same shape? If yes, restructure at least one.

---

## 15. Repetition Disguised as Depth

**Pattern:** Restating the same point in different words across paragraphs. Each paragraph must earn its place by adding new information, a sharper claim, a tighter example, a counterpoint, or a consequence.

**Test:** Can you delete this paragraph without the reader losing any new information? If yes, cut it.

---

## 16. Sentence-Initial Conjunctions

**Pattern:** Starting sentences with "But" or "And." This is a grammatical error that also tends to create the false-punchy, fragmented feel of AI prose.

**Bad:** "But the engine? That's a different story."
**Good:** "The engine, however, is a different story."

**What to do instead:** Use "However," "Still," "Yet," or merge the sentence with the one before it.

---

## 17. Contrastive Reframing (Antithesis Overuse)

**Pattern:** Sentences or pairs structured as "not X, but Y" or "X doesn't do this — it does that." Used once for emphasis, this is a legitimate rhetorical move. Used repeatedly across an essay, it becomes a tic that signals AI-generated prose.

**Kill list constructions:** "Not X, but Y." / "It's not X. It's Y." / "X doesn't [verb]. It [verbs]." / "Less X, more Y."

**Test:** Count the number of contrastive reframings in the draft. More than two in a 1,500-word essay is overuse. Pick the one that earns it most and rewrite the others as positive statements.

**What to do instead:** State the positive claim directly.

---

## How to Use This Checklist

1. **During review:** Run through every item mechanically. Flag each instance with a line number.
2. **During coaching (`governance-coach`):** Reference specific patterns when giving voice feedback.
3. **When editing:** When a flagged pattern appears, suggest a specific rewrite.
4. **Self-editing:** Read your draft aloud. AI tells are easier to hear than to see.

This list will grow. When you catch a new pattern in your own drafts, add it here.

## Русский блок (Telegram)

Английский список выше почти не переносится на русский буквально (кроме
пп. 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 15, 16, 17 — конструкции, которые звучат по-русски
так же формально). Отдельные русские штампы:

### Р1. Канцелярит и вводные обороты
**Стоп-лист:** «на сегодняшний день», «в современных условиях», «нельзя не
отметить», «стоит подчеркнуть», «важно понимать, что», «как известно»,
«не секрет, что».
**Вместо этого:** сразу факт, без разгона.

### Р2. Ложная тройка
Как и в английском — три пункта просто потому, что «принято» (например,
«риски, вызовы и возможности»), когда по факту есть только один или два.

### Р3. Уравновешенное «с одной стороны / с другой стороны»
Отказ от позиции через искусственный баланс. Если позиция есть — займи её,
не размывай двумя равными половинками абзаца.

### Р4. Пустое резюмирование в конце
«Таким образом, можно сделать вывод, что...» — переформулирует уже сказанное
без нового смысла. Вывод поста — конкретный вопрос/действие, не пересказ.

### Р5. Дежурное «давайте разберёмся» / «поговорим о том, что»
Анонс темы вместо перехода прямо к сути — тот же грех, что и английский
Signpost Phrases (п. 6 выше).

**Не проверяй по формальному совпадению слов — оценивай, звучит ли фраза
как живой профессионал КУ или как шаблон.**
